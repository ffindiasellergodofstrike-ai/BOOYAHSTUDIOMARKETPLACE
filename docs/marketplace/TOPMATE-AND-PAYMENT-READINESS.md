## Settlement and dispute risk that automation cannot remove

Cashfree's current [merchant terms](https://www.cashfree.com/tnc), updated 10 March 2026, permit withholding for high chargeback/refund risk or delivery failures. Schedule A, clause 17(7), states a termination right when the **chargeback-to-gross-transaction-value ratio exceeds 1%**. That is not automatically the same as a 1% count of UPI complaints. Ask Cashfree to confirm the applicable contract, calculation window and what events enter the numerator. Track both case count and disputed value; do not label a dashboard estimate the provider's contractual metric. The same terms require accurate business disclosure and do not imply approval when the business model changes.

A predicted 2–3% dispute count is a material underwriting concern, not a safe operating allowance. Evidence automation can capture genuine payment, policy acceptance, email and access records, refresh known cases and surface deadlines. It cannot guarantee a win, remove an unauthorized-payment complaint, force settlement or override bank/provider risk controls. Never automatically call every complainant fraudulent or treat a signed URL as proof that the cardholder authorized payment.

Practical controls are accurate previews/requirements/licenses, recognizable merchant descriptors, support before escalation, prompt fixes/refunds for valid failures, seller and file review, monitoring by seller/product/provider, and adequate liquidity for legitimate refunds. After a provider suspension, switching gateways must not be used to conceal the risk or misrepresent the business to another provider.

## Live approval packet and remaining operational checks

Before enabling third-party live checkout, prepare these concrete materials:

1. Owner identity/KYC, proof of business activity/address and matching bank details requested by the provider; the real domain and accurate operator identity.
2. A working demo of registration, product detail, price/license, checkout, verified payment, email receipt, private downloads, support/refund and cancellation.
3. Buyer terms and the platform–creator agreement with recorded acceptance; a specimen seller/product/order snapshot and the actual commission/fee arrangement.
4. An honest money-flow diagram identifying buyer, platform, provider and creator; company-owned sales versus third-party sales; who supplies tax documents; refund and dispute liability; provider-approved settlement flow.
5. Seller onboarding and required **public business identity/customer-care/grievance disclosures**, distinct from private PAN/bank/KYC. A display name alone may be insufficient. Do not publish private identity documents as a shortcut.
6. Provider marketplace approval, vendor activation proof, webhook endpoints, sandbox evidence and agreed live/recurring activation. Environment flags are deployment controls, not independent proof of approval.
7. A functioning complaint intake/acknowledgment process, refund handling, dispute deadlines, accessible copyright/takedown reporting and retention/deletion procedure. Maintain actual last-run monitoring for scheduled jobs.

The [government-hosted consolidated E-Commerce Rules](https://thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Rules/Consumer%20Protection%20(E-Commerce)%20Rules,%202020.pdf) cover affirmative purchase consent, seller undertakings, seller disclosures and grievance duties. Have their application to this digital marketplace checked before launch; provider approval is separate from those obligations.

There is also a dated upcoming change: the [official government announcement of 10 September 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=294532) says the 2026 amendments take effect **1 January 2027**. It identifies NCH convergence, a copy of recorded complaints, transparent search/sponsored results, a 30-day-lowest-price basis for announced reductions, and dark-pattern compliance/self-audit requirements. This source review does not certify those future operational measures as completed. Verify the notification and implement applicable measures before that effective date; avoid invented price discounts or claims of an audit certificate.
